\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
%=====================================================================

The arithmetization presents $N$ columns and asks for evaluations of them. Column $i$ is a
multilinear $P_i:\cube{k_i}\to\F$, of logical length $2^{k_i}$, but only a prefix of
$h_i\le2^{k_i}$ entries carries data: from $h_i$ on it repeats a fixed public value $p_i$.
The $h_i$ are run-time quantities while the $k_i$ are fixed when the circuit is, so the
padding is real waste, and committing the columns one by one costs the verifier work linear
in $N$ on top of it.

Commit instead, once, to the concatenation of the real prefixes with the padding left out: a
dense $q:\cube m\to\F$ with $2^m$ near $\sum_i h_i$. Each column then occupies a known
interval of the dense cube, and what is left to build is a reduction carrying a claim
$\pol{P_i}(\zeta)=v$ at a point $\zeta\in\F^{k_i}$ to a weighted claim $\inner Wq=C$ on
that single
commitment, with $W$ fixed by the interval and evaluable by the verifier in $O(m)$, plus the
column's pad value. This is the jagged PCS of~\cite{jagged}.

Section~\ref{sec:layout} fixes the layout and Section~\ref{sec:protocol} states the
reduction, in full, as one protocol: the padding is discharged in its first step and the
whole batch of claims collapses to a single weighted claim in its third. The two sections
after it are the evaluation of $W$, which is where all the work is. It is the multilinear
extension of an interval indicator, computed by a width-four read-once branching
program~\cite{jagged} (Section~\ref{sec:automaton}); and what the PCS asks for is
not one evaluation of it but a contraction against the tail of its sumcheck
(Section~\ref{sec:contract}).

Two things separate this from the original paper~\cite{jagged}. Our PCS opens an arbitrary
verifier-evaluable weighted claim, so the adapter's $m$-round reduction sumcheck (about
$5\cdot2^m$ prover multiplications, reducing $\inner Wq=C$ to one evaluation of $\pol q$)
is unnecessary
and the jagged indicator is itself the opening weight. And our PCS is
BaseFold/WHIR-style~\cite{ligerito,whir}: it stops its sumcheck with $L$ variables left and
transmits the tail in the clear, which is where the contraction comes from.

Notation, multilinear extensions and the inner-product commitment interface are those of the
main document: an opening proves $\inner Wq=\sum_{w\in\cube m}W(w)q(w)=C$ against any weight
$W$ the verifier can evaluate, a point evaluation being $W=\eq(\zeta,\cdot)$. Bit strings are
little-endian, $\val a=\sum_j a_j2^j$, and the field has characteristic two, so $1-x=1+x$.

%=====================================================================
\section{The commitment}\label{sec:layout}
%=====================================================================

Columns of equal height that are always claimed together are grouped into \emph{blocks} of
power-of-two width. A group of $g$ such columns is cut greedily ($g=9$ becomes $8+1$), so no
padding column is introduced. A block of width $B=2^b$ whose columns
$P_c:\cube k\to\F$, $c\in\cube b$, have logical dimension $k$ and common real height
$h\le2^k$ is stored row-major at an integer offset $t\ge0$:
\begin{equation}
  q\bigl(t+\val c+B\val z\bigr)=P_c(z)+p_c,\qquad c\in\cube b,\ z\in\cube k,\ \val z<h,
  \label{eq:place}
\end{equation}
so the block occupies the interval $[t,e)$ with $e=t+Bh$. What is stored is the column
offset by its own pad value $p_c$, the constant the arithmetization reads at and above $h$.
The offset column vanishes there, so it is still supported exactly on the committed prefix,
and Section~\ref{sec:protocol} shows that the offset costs the verifier one field addition
in place of an evaluation. Blocks are concatenated with no
alignment gap, and only the last is followed by zero padding, up to
\[
  m=\lceil\log_2 A\rceil,\qquad A=\sum_{\text{blocks}}Bh .
\]
The endpoints are public: they are the prefix sums of the block lengths $Bh$, never
transmitted, and a circuit verifier forms them with a ripple-carry adder at one
multiplication per bit, the carry out of a position with addend bits $\sigma,\eta$ and carry
in $\kappa$ being $\maj(\sigma,\eta,\kappa)=(\sigma+\kappa)(\sigma+\eta)+\sigma$.

%=====================================================================
\section{The protocol}\label{sec:protocol}
%=====================================================================

The protocol is built from two weights, which we define first. Given integer bounds
$0\le t\le e\le2^m$ and a family of \emph{row weights}
$u=(u_j^0,u_j^1)_{j<n}\in(\F^2)^n$, the \emph{interval weight}
$W^{t,e}_u:\cube m\to\F$ is
\begin{equation}
  W^{t,e}_u(w):=\sum_{\substack{a\in\cube n\\ t+\val a<e}}\Bigl(\prod_{j<n}u_j^{a_j}\Bigr)\bigl[\val w=t+\val a\bigr],
  \label{eq:weight}
\end{equation}

One row-weight family serves a whole block. For a block of width $B=2^b$ and dimension
$k$, claimed at a point $\zeta\in\F^k$, and a batching scalar $\gamma\in\F$, define
$u^{\star}(\gamma,\zeta)\in(\F^2)^{b+k}$, so that $n=b+k$, by
\begin{equation}
  u^{\star}(\gamma,\zeta)_j:=\bigl(1,\,\gamma^{2^j}\bigr)\ \ (j<b),\qquad u^{\star}(\gamma,\zeta)_{b+i}:=(1+\zeta_i,\,\zeta_i)\ \ (i<k).
  \label{eq:ustar}
\end{equation}
At $b=0$ the selector part is empty and $u^{\star}$ is the plain equality family
$(1+\zeta_i,\zeta_i)_{i<k}$.

\begin{protocol}[Jagged opening]\label{prot:jagged}
\ \par\smallskip
\emph{Public:} the blocks and their integer intervals $[t,e)$ from
Section~\ref{sec:layout}, each block's width $B=2^b$, dimension $k$, height $h$ and per-slot
pad values $p_c\in\F$, $c\in\cube b$.

\smallskip
\textbf{Commit.} The prover sends one dense commitment to $q:\cube m\to\F$ built
by~\eqref{eq:place}. Nothing else is committed.

\smallskip
\textbf{Open}, given the arithmetization's claims
$\pol{P^{\mathrm{log}}_c}(\zeta_c)=v_c$ at points $\zeta_c\in\F^k$ with values $v_c\in\F$, one
per column, where $P^{\mathrm{log}}_c:\cube k\to\F$ agrees with $P_c$ below $h$ and equals
the public $p_c$ at and above $h$:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item \emph{(pad)} Both parties correct each claimed value to $v_c':=v_c+p_c\in\F$.
\item \emph{(rank)} The verifier observes every $v_c'$ and samples $\gamma\leftarrow\F$. Fix
      any public order of the blocks and number the claims $0,1,2,\dots$ along it, each
      block's $B$ claims consecutively and in slot order. The number a block's slot $0^b$
      receives is its \emph{base rank} $\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}$, and its slot $c$ then has
      $\mathrm{rk}(c)=\beta+\val c$.
\item \emph{(batch)} Both parties form the single weighted claim
      $(W,C)\in\F^{\cube m}\times\F$,
      \[
        W:=\sum_{\text{blocks}}\gamma^{\beta}\,W^{t,e}_{u^{\star}(\gamma,\zeta)},
        \qquad C:=\sum_c\gamma^{\mathrm{rk}(c)}\,v_c' .
      \]
\item \emph{(open)} The PCS opens $\inner Wq=C$ against the commitment.
\end{enumerate}
\end{protocol}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:complete}
If every input claim holds then $\inner Wq=C$. Conversely, for any fixed $q$, if some input
claim fails then $\inner Wq\neq C$ except with probability $(\text{number of claims})/|\F|$
over $\gamma$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Introduce, for one slot $c\in\cube b$ of a block claimed at $\zeta$, the per-slot family
$u(c,\zeta)\in(\F^2)^{b+k}$,
\begin{equation}
  u(c,\zeta)_j:=(1+c_j,\,c_j)\ \ (j<b),\qquad u(c,\zeta)_{b+i}:=(1+\zeta_i,\,\zeta_i)\ \ (i<k),
  \label{eq:uslot}
\end{equation}
which appears nowhere in the protocol and only here. The proof rests on two identities.
The first concerns a single slot. Write $R_c:=P^{\mathrm{log}}_c+p_c$, which
by~\eqref{eq:place} is what the commitment holds on $[0,h)$ and which vanishes at and above
$h$. The multilinear extension of a constant is that constant, so
$\pol{P^{\mathrm{log}}_c}(\zeta)=\pol{R_c}(\zeta)+p_c$ at every $\zeta$, independently of
$h$; and expanding $\pol{R_c}$ over its support, using $\val c+B\val z=\val{(c,z)}$,
\begin{equation}
  \pol{P^{\mathrm{log}}_c}(\zeta)=\sum_{\val z<h}\eq(\zeta,z)\,q\bigl(t+\val{(c,z)}\bigr)+p_c
  =\bigl\langle W^{t,e}_{u(c,\zeta)},q\bigr\rangle+p_c,
  \label{eq:split}
\end{equation}
because $\prod_{j<b}u(c,\zeta)_j^{a_j}=[a_{<b}=c]$ selects $a=(c,z)$, the remaining factor is
$\eq(\zeta,z)$, and $t+\val a<e=t+Bh$ is exactly $\val z<h$ once $\val c<B$. So step~1
makes $v_c'-\inner{W^{t,e}_{u(c,\zeta_c)}}q$ the discrepancy of the claim on column $c$, zero
exactly when that claim holds.

The second identity concerns a complete block. Fix $a\in\cube{b+k}$ in~\eqref{eq:weight}. Its
selector factor under $u(c,\zeta)$ is $[a_{<b}=c]$, so summing over the $B$ slots with their
consecutive ranks gives
\[
  \sum_{c\in\cube b}\gamma^{\beta+\val c}\prod_{j<b}u(c,\zeta)_j^{a_j}
  =\gamma^{\beta+\val{a_{<b}}}=\gamma^{\beta}\prod_{j<b}\bigl(\gamma^{2^j}\bigr)^{a_j},
\]
which is $\gamma^\beta$ times the selector factor of $u^{\star}(\gamma,\zeta)$; the row
coordinates are identical. Hence
$\sum_{c}\gamma^{\beta+\val c}W^{t,e}_{u(c,\zeta)}=\gamma^{\beta}W^{t,e}_{u^{\star}(\gamma,\zeta)}$,
so summing that over the blocks shows $W$ of step~3 equals
$\sum_c\gamma^{\mathrm{rk}(c)}W^{t,e}_{u(c,\zeta_c)}$, one term per claim. Completeness
follows. For the converse, $\inner Wq-C=\sum_c\gamma^{\mathrm{rk}(c)}\bigl(\inner{W^{t,e}_{u(c,\zeta_c)}}q-v_c'\bigr)$
is a nonzero polynomial in $\gamma$ of degree below the number of claims.
\end{proof}

Nothing else is proved about the reduction: it is an identity, and the soundness of the
opening is that of the PCS, degraded only by the $\gamma$ term above. One evaluation is left
for the verifier, $\pol W$, and the rest of the note is about it.

%=====================================================================
\section{The interval weight is a branching program}\label{sec:automaton}
%=====================================================================

Fix $t,e,u$ as in~\eqref{eq:weight}, extend $u$ by $u_j:=(1,0)\in\F^2$ for
$n\le j\le m$, and fix an evaluation point $x\in\F^m$, extended by $x_m:=0$. The automaton
reads positions $j=0,\dots,m$, least significant first, carrying a mass vector $S\in\F^Q$
over the four-element state set
\[
  Q:=\{0,1,2,3\},\qquad \text{state }\kappa+2\lambda\text{ meaning }(\kappa,\lambda),\ \ \kappa,\lambda\in\{0,1\},
\]
where $\kappa$ is the carry of $\val w=t+\val a$ over the bits read and $\lambda$ is the
comparison $\val w<e$ over those bits (a more significant differing bit overrides a less
significant one, so reading upward each differing bit overwrites $\lambda$). Two registers
suffice, so the program has width four~\cite{jagged}.

At position $j$ let $t_j,e_j\in\{0,1\}$ be the $j$-th bits of $t$ and $e$, and write
$\chi_j^0:=1+x_j$ and $\chi_j^1:=x_j$ in $\F$ for the two per-coordinate factors of $\eq$, so
that $\eq(\varepsilon,x)=\prod_j\chi_j^{\varepsilon_j}$ for Boolean $\varepsilon$. Set
\[
  g_0:=u_j^0\chi_j^0,\quad g_1:=u_j^1\chi_j^0,\quad g_2:=u_j^0\chi_j^1,\quad g_3:=u_j^1\chi_j^1 .
\]
Reading a row bit $a$ and an index bit $\omega$ from $(\kappa,\lambda)$ is permitted only
when $\omega=a\oplus\kappa\oplus t_j$, and then
\[
  \kappa'=\maj(a,\kappa,t_j),\qquad
  \lambda'=\begin{cases}\lambda & \omega=e_j,\\ e_j & \omega\neq e_j,\end{cases}
  \qquad\text{with weight } u_j^a\chi_j^{\omega}.
\]
The output is $S_2$ after position $m$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:automaton}
For every $x\in\F^m$ the automaton outputs
\[
  \sum_{\substack{a\in\cube n\\ t+\val a<e}}\Bigl(\prod_{j<n}u_j^{a_j}\Bigr)\eq\bigl(t+\val a,\,x\bigr)\;=\;\pol{W^{t,e}_u}(x).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Expanding the product of transition matrices, a path is a choice of $(a_j,\omega_j)_{j\le m}$
of weight $\prod_j u_j^{a_j}\chi_j^{\omega_j}$. The constraint
$\omega_j=a_j\oplus\kappa_j\oplus t_j$ with $\kappa_{j+1}=\maj(a_j,\kappa_j,t_j)$,
$\kappa_0=0$, is binary addition, so paths biject with choices of $a$ and satisfy
$\val\omega=t+\val a$; then $\prod_j\chi_j^{\omega_j}=\eq(\omega,x)$, and $u_j=(1,0)$ for
$j\ge n$ kills every $a$ with a bit at or above $n$. Acceptance is $t+\val a<e$: $\lambda$
ends at $1$ exactly when the most significant position where $\omega$ and $e$ differ has
$e$-bit $1$, and a path that carries into position $m$ is annihilated there, because the
carry forces $\omega_m=1$, of weight $\chi_m^1=x_m=0$. That extra position also represents
$e=2^m$: there $e_m=1$, which sets $\lambda$ to $1$. Both sides are multilinear in every coordinate of $x$ and agree at
Boolean arguments, hence agree everywhere.
\end{proof}

Since $t_j,e_j$ are public bits, the step selects one of four fixed matrices instead of
evaluating a tensor over $(a,\omega)$. With $S$ incoming and $S'$ outgoing:
\[
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.25}
\begin{array}{l@{\qquad}l}
(t_j,e_j)=(0,0): &
\begin{aligned}
S_0'&=S_0(g_0+g_3)+(S_1+S_3)g_2+S_2g_3, & S_1'&=S_1g_1,\\
S_2'&=S_2g_0, & S_3'&=S_3g_1,
\end{aligned}\\[2.2ex]
(t_j,e_j)=(0,1): &
\begin{aligned}
S_0'&=S_0g_3+S_1g_2, & S_1'&=0,\\
S_2'&=S_0g_0+S_2(g_0+g_3)+S_3g_2, & S_3'&=(S_1+S_3)g_1,
\end{aligned}\\[2.2ex]
(t_j,e_j)=(1,0): &
\begin{aligned}
S_0'&=(S_0+S_2)g_2, & S_1'&=S_0g_1+S_1(g_0+g_3)+S_3g_3,\\
S_2'&=0, & S_3'&=S_2g_1+S_3g_0,
\end{aligned}\\[2.2ex]
(t_j,e_j)=(1,1): &
\begin{aligned}
S_0'&=S_0g_2, & S_1'&=S_1g_3,\\
S_2'&=S_2g_2, & S_3'&=(S_0+S_2)g_1+S_1g_0+S_3(g_0+g_3).
\end{aligned}
\end{array}
\]
Each arm costs $6$ multiplications. The $g_i$ cost one more. At a row coordinate, where
$u_j=(1+r,r)$ for that coordinate's value $r\in\F$, form $\pi:=rx_j$ and read off
$g_0=1+r+x_j+\pi$, $g_1=r+\pi$, $g_2=x_j+\pi$, $g_3=\pi$; at a selector coordinate, where
$u_j=(1,\gamma^{2^j})$, form $\pi:=\gamma^{2^j}x_j$ and read off $g_0=1+x_j$,
$g_1=\gamma^{2^j}+\pi$, $g_2=x_j$, $g_3=\pi$. A position therefore costs $7$
multiplications and a run $7(m+1)$, plus one squaring per selector position to advance
$\gamma^{2^j}$. One run per
\emph{block}, not per column: that is what step~2 of Protocol~\ref{prot:jagged} buys, and it
is why~\eqref{eq:weight} was stated for weights that need not sum to $1$. Normalising $u^{\star}$, that is
writing $(1,\gamma^{2^j})=(1+\gamma^{2^j})\cdot(1+\theta_j,\,\theta_j)$ with
$\theta_j=\gamma^{2^j}/(1+\gamma^{2^j})$, would cost $b$ inversions and fail at $\gamma=1$.

\section{What the verifier evaluates}\label{sec:contract}
%=====================================================================

A BaseFold/WHIR-style opening never evaluates $\pol W$ at a point. It consumes $m-L$ of the
$m$ variables in sumcheck rounds, leaving a challenge prefix $\rho\in\F^{m-L}$, and
transmits the remaining $L$-variable message $f:\cube L\to\F$ in the clear, $L$
being small (typically $3$ or $4$). Its last check needs
\begin{equation}
  \Sigma:=\sum_{y\in\cube L}f(y)\,\pol W(\rho,y).
  \label{eq:contract}
\end{equation}
Running the automaton $2^L$ times costs $2^L\cdot7(m+1)$ per block. One run suffices.

\paragraph{Matrix form.} Collect the transitions above into $T_j(x_j)\in\F^{Q\times Q}$,
with $\bigl(T_j(x_j)\bigr)_{s,s'}$ the weight of $s\to s'$, so that a state row vector
updates by right multiplication. Then
\[
  \pol W(x)=\Bigl(\prod_{j=0}^{m}T_j(x_j)\Bigr)_{0,2}
          =\Bigl[\Bigl(\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}T_j(x_j)\Bigr)\mathrm{acc}\Bigr]_{0},
  \qquad \mathrm{acc}:=\text{column }2\text{ of }T_m(0)\ \in\F^Q ,
\]
that is, entry $(0,2)$ of the whole product, or entry $0$ of all but the last matrix applied
to the acceptance column. The transition rule at $j=m$ (where $a_m=\omega_m=0$ and $t_m=0$)
gives
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{acc}=(\,e_m,\ 0,\ 1,\ 0\,),
  \label{eq:accept}
\end{equation}
which is the indicator of state $2$ unless the block ends exactly at $e=2^m$. Splitting the
rest at $j=m-L$ and letting $S$ be row $0$ of $\prod_{j<m-L}T_j(\rho_j)$,
\begin{equation}
  \Sigma=\inner SV,\qquad
  V:=\sum_{y\in\cube L}f(y)\Bigl(\prod_{j=m-L}^{m-1}T_j\bigl(y_{j-(m-L)}\bigr)\Bigr)\mathrm{acc} .
  \label{eq:SV}
\end{equation}
Here $S,V\in\F^Q$, and $S$ is one automaton run of $m-L$ steps. $V$ is a reverse dynamic
program over vectors $V_j(y')\in\F^Q$ indexed by the residual bits $y'\in\cube{\,j-(m-L)}$
below position $j$, with $V=V_{m-L}$ on the empty index. The residual index coordinates are
Boolean, so $T_j$ collapses to $T_j^{(0)}$ or $T_j^{(1)}$, and
\[
  V_j(y')=T_j^{(0)}V_{j+1}(y',0)+T_j^{(1)}V_{j+1}(y',1),\qquad V_m(y)=f(y)\,\mathrm{acc},
\]
peeling the most significant residual bit first: $2^L-1$ reverse steps, then $\inner SV$.

\paragraph{The reverse step.} It takes the two children $V^{0}:=V_{j+1}(y',0)$ and
$V^{1}:=V_{j+1}(y',1)$ in $\F^Q$, the row coordinate $r$, and $t_j,e_j$. Transposing the
transitions with the row pair $u_j=(1+r,r)$ gives, for $(t_j,e_j)=(0,0)$,
\[
  V'=\bigl((1+r)V^{0}_0+rV^{1}_0,\ \ rV^{0}_1+(1+r)V^{1}_0,\ \ (1+r)V^{0}_2+rV^{1}_0,\ \ rV^{0}_3+(1+r)V^{1}_0\bigr),
\]
and analogously for the other three: $8$ multiplications. For $r\in\{0,1\}$ the step is a
pure selection and costs nothing: $(V^{0}_0,V^{1}_0,V^{0}_2,V^{1}_0)$ at $r=0$, and
$(V^{1}_0,V^{0}_1,V^{1}_0,V^{0}_3)$ at $r=1$. The row point is zero above a column's logical dimension, so the free branch fires on
every residual coordinate the column does not use.

\paragraph{Closed form when all residual row coordinates vanish.} With the high row bits
pinned to zero, $\val w=t+\val a$ forces $w$'s high part to be
the integer $t_{\mathrm{hi}}:=\lfloor t/2^{m-L}\rfloor$ plus the incoming carry, so each
state reaches exactly one message index. With $e_{\mathrm{hi}}:=\lfloor e/2^{m-L}\rfloor$
read off the $L+1$ top bits of $e$ (the extra bit covering $e=2^m$),
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \Sigma=\ &f(t_{\mathrm{hi}})\Bigl(S_{S_0}[\,t_{\mathrm{hi}}<e_{\mathrm{hi}}\,]+S_{S_2}[\,t_{\mathrm{hi}}\le e_{\mathrm{hi}}\,]\Bigr)\\
  +\ &f(t_{\mathrm{hi}}{+}1)\Bigl(S_{S_1}[\,t_{\mathrm{hi}}{+}1<e_{\mathrm{hi}}\,]+S_{S_3}[\,t_{\mathrm{hi}}{+}1\le e_{\mathrm{hi}}\,]\Bigr).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:closed}
\end{equation}
The high part decides $\val w<e$ when it differs from $e_{\mathrm{hi}}$; when it agrees the
prefix comparison decides, so $S_0$ (with $\lambda=0$) contributes only under a strict $<$
while $S_2$ (with $\lambda=1$) also contributes at equality, and likewise for $S_1,S_3$
at $t_{\mathrm{hi}}+1$. This costs $O(L)$, namely a ripple increment of $t_{\mathrm{hi}}$
and one MSB-first pass yielding $[\,t_{\mathrm{hi}}<e_{\mathrm{hi}}\,]$, $[\,t_{\mathrm{hi}}=e_{\mathrm{hi}}\,]$
and $[\,e_{\mathrm{hi}}=t_{\mathrm{hi}}{+}1\,]$ (the last converting $<$ into
$t_{\mathrm{hi}}{+}1<e_{\mathrm{hi}}$ via
$[\,t_{\mathrm{hi}}<e_{\mathrm{hi}}\,]\cdot(1+[\,e_{\mathrm{hi}}=t_{\mathrm{hi}}{+}1\,])$),
and two message reads. Address $f$ with one zero sentinel after its $2^L$ entries
so that a carry out of the last index reads in range.

\begin{center}
\fbox{\parbox{0.93\textwidth}{
\textbf{Terminal evaluation of one block's weight.}
\emph{Input:} bits of $t,e$; width $b$; scalar $\gamma$ and base rank $\beta$; row point
$\zeta$, zero-padded; round challenges $\rho\in\F^{m-L}$; final message $f$.
\emph{Output:} the block's term of~\eqref{eq:contract}.
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1pt]
\item Run the automaton over $j=0,\dots,m-L-1$ at coordinates $\rho_j$ with the weights
$u^{\star}(\gamma,\zeta)$ of~\eqref{eq:ustar}, giving $S\in\F^Q$. Cost $7(m-L)$.
\item If $\zeta$ vanishes at every residual coordinate, return
$\gamma^{\beta}\cdot$\eqref{eq:closed}. Cost $O(L)$.
\item Otherwise set $V(y)=f(y)\,\mathrm{acc}$ for $y\in\cube L$, apply one reverse step
to each pair for $j=m-1$ down to $m-L$, and return $\gamma^{\beta}\inner SV$. Cost at most
$8(2^L-1)+4$, and zero per step whose row coordinate is Boolean.
\end{enumerate}
}}
\end{center}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:topbit}
Step~3 is exact with $\mathrm{acc}$ taken to be the indicator of state $2$, which
holds only when the block does not end at $2^m$;
otherwise~\eqref{eq:accept} must be used. Step~2 consults that bit through
$e_{\mathrm{hi}}$, so the closed form is unconditional.
\end{remark}

%=====================================================================
\section{Cost}\label{sec:cost}
%=====================================================================

\emph{Prover.} One commitment to $q$, of length $2^m$ within a factor of two of the real
area, and no second oracle. The only work specific to the adapter is building $W$ over
$\cube m$: one $\eq$ table per distinct opening point, then one multiply-accumulate per
(cell, claim) pair into that claim's interval.

\emph{Verifier.} Per block, $7(m-L)$ multiplications for the automaton prefix, at most
$8(2^L-1)+4$ for the contraction, and one addition per claim for the padding, so
roughly $7m$
multiplications per \emph{block} rather than per column. The
computation is straight-line in $m$ and the block count, with the shape of the data entering
only as bits, which is what makes it cheap to prove recursively.
