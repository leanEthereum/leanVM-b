\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
%=====================================================================

The arithmetization presents $N$ columns and asks for evaluations of them. Column $i$ is a
multilinear $P_i$ on $\cube{k_i}$, of logical length $2^{k_i}$, but only a prefix of
$h_i\le2^{k_i}$ entries carries data: from $h_i$ on it repeats a fixed public value $p_i$.
The $h_i$ are run-time quantities while the $k_i$ are fixed when the circuit is, so the
padding is real waste, and committing the columns one by one costs the verifier work linear
in $N$ on top of it.

Commit instead, once, to the concatenation of the real prefixes with the padding left out: a
dense $q:\cube m\to\F$ with $2^m$ near $\sum_i h_i$. Each column then occupies a known
interval of the dense cube, and what is left to build is a reduction carrying a claim
$\pol{P_i}(\zeta)=v$ on a padded column to a weighted claim $\inner Wq=C$ on that single
commitment, with $W$ fixed by the interval and evaluable by the verifier in $O(m)$, plus a
padding term the verifier computes for itself. This is the jagged PCS of~\cite{jagged}.

Section~\ref{sec:layout} fixes the layout and Section~\ref{sec:protocol} states the
reduction, in full, as one protocol: the padding is discharged in its first step and the
whole batch of claims collapses to a single weighted claim in its third. The two sections
after it are the evaluation of $W$, which is where all the work is. It is the multilinear
extension of an interval indicator, computed by a width-four read-once branching
program~\cite{jagged} (Section~\ref{sec:automaton}); and what the PCS asks for is
not one evaluation of it but a contraction against the tail of its sumcheck
(Section~\ref{sec:contract}).

Two things separate this from the original paper~\cite{jagged}. Our PCS opens an arbitrary
verifier-evaluable weighted claim, so the adapter's $m$-round reduction sumcheck (about
$5\cdot2^m$ prover multiplications, reducing $\inner Wq=C$ to $\pol q(\rho)$) is unnecessary
and the jagged indicator is itself the opening weight. And our PCS is
BaseFold/WHIR-style~\cite{ligerito,whir}: it stops its sumcheck with $L$ variables left and
transmits the tail in the clear, which is where the contraction comes from.

Notation, multilinear extensions and the inner-product commitment interface are those of the
main document: an opening proves $\inner Wq=\sum_{w\in\cube m}W(w)q(w)=C$ against any weight
$W$ the verifier can evaluate, a point evaluation being $W=\eq(r,\cdot)$. Bit strings are
little-endian, $\val a=\sum_j a_j2^j$, and the field has characteristic two, so $1-x=1+x$.

%=====================================================================
\section{The commitment}\label{sec:layout}
%=====================================================================

Columns of equal height that are always claimed together are grouped into \emph{blocks} of
power-of-two width. A group of $g$ such columns is cut greedily ($g=9$ becomes $8+1$), so no
padding column is introduced. A block of width $B=2^b$ whose columns $P_c$, $c\in\cube b$,
have logical dimension $k$ and common real height $h\le2^k$ is stored row-major at a dense
offset $t$:
\begin{equation}
  q\bigl(t+c+B\val z\bigr)=P_c(z),\qquad c\in\cube b,\ z\in\cube k,\ \val z<h,
  \label{eq:place}
\end{equation}
so the block occupies the interval $[t,e)$ with $e=t+Bh$. Blocks are concatenated with no
alignment gap, and only the last is followed by zero padding, up to
\[
  m=\max\Bigl(\lceil\log_2 A\rceil,\ \max_{\text{blocks}}(k+b)\Bigr),\qquad A=\sum_{\text{blocks}}Bh .
\]
The second term guarantees that every block's logical cube $\cube{k+b}$ embeds in the dense
cube even when its physical interval is short. The endpoints are public: they are the prefix
sums of the announced heights, computed by a ripple-carry adder (one multiplication per bit,
the carry being $\maj(s,h,\kappa)=(s+\kappa)(s+h)+s$), never transmitted.

%=====================================================================
\section{The protocol}\label{sec:protocol}
%=====================================================================

Two weights. For public $0\le t\le e\le2^m$ and \emph{row weights} $u=(u_j^0,u_j^1)_{j<n}$,
$n\le m$, the \emph{interval weight} $W^{t,e}_u:\cube m\to\F$ is
\begin{equation}
  W^{t,e}_u(w):=\sum_{\substack{a\in\cube n\\ t+\val a<e}}\Bigl(\prod_{j<n}u_j^{a_j}\Bigr)\bigl[\val w=t+\val a\bigr],
  \label{eq:weight}
\end{equation}
and for $h\le2^k$ the \emph{prefix indicator} is $H_h:\cube k\to\F$, $H_h(z):=[\val z<h]$.
On a block of width $B=2^b$ two row-weight families occur, both on $n=b+k$ coordinates: for a
single slot $c\in\cube b$ claimed at $\zeta\in\F^k$,
\begin{equation}
  u(c,\zeta)_j:=(1+c_j,\,c_j)\ \ (j<b),\qquad u(c,\zeta)_{b+i}:=(1+\zeta_i,\,\zeta_i)\ \ (i<k),
  \label{eq:uslot}
\end{equation}
and for all $B$ slots at once, given a batching scalar $\gamma$,
\begin{equation}
  u^{\star}(\gamma,\zeta)_j:=\bigl(1,\,\gamma^{2^j}\bigr)\ \ (j<b),\qquad u^{\star}(\gamma,\zeta)_{b+i}:=(1+\zeta_i,\,\zeta_i)\ \ (i<k).
  \label{eq:ustar}
\end{equation}
The selector pairs of $u^{\star}$ do not sum to $1$; \eqref{eq:weight} does not require it.

\begin{protocol}[Jagged opening]\label{prot:jagged}
\ \par\smallskip
\emph{Public:} the blocks and their intervals $[t,e)$ from Section~\ref{sec:layout}, each
block's width $B=2^b$, dimension $k$, height $h$ and per-slot pad value $p_c$.

\smallskip
\textbf{Commit.} $\mathbf P$ sends one dense commitment to $q:\cube m\to\F$ built
by~\eqref{eq:place}. Nothing else is committed.

\smallskip
\textbf{Open}, given the arithmetization's claims $\pol{P^{\mathrm{log}}_c}(\zeta_c)=v_c$,
one per column, where $P^{\mathrm{log}}_c$ is $P_c$ below $h$ and the public $p_c$ at and
above $h$:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item \emph{(pad)} Both parties replace each claimed value by
      $C_c:=v_c+p_c\bigl(1+\pol{H_h}(\zeta_c)\bigr)$, the verifier evaluating $\pol{H_h}$
      by~\eqref{eq:prefix} below.
\item \emph{(rank)} $\mathbf V$ observes every $C_c$, then samples $\gamma\leftarrow\F$ and
      fixes a rank per claim: a block whose $B$ claims share one row point $\zeta$ and cover
      all $B$ slots receives the consecutive ranks $\beta+\val c$ in slot order; every
      other claim receives a rank of its own.
\item \emph{(batch)} Both parties form the single weighted claim $(W,C)$,
      \[
        W:=\sum_{\text{complete blocks}}\gamma^{\beta}\,W^{t,e}_{u^{\star}(\gamma,\zeta)}
           \;+\;\sum_{\text{other claims}}\gamma^{\mathrm{rk}}\,W^{t,e}_{u(c,\zeta_c)},
        \qquad C:=\sum_c\gamma^{\mathrm{rk}(c)}\,C_c .
      \]
\item \emph{(open)} The PCS opens $\inner Wq=C$ against the commitment.
\end{enumerate}
\end{protocol}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:complete}
If every input claim holds then $\inner Wq=C$. Conversely, for any fixed $q$, if some input
claim fails then $\inner Wq\neq C$ except with probability $(\text{number of claims})/|\F|$
over $\gamma$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Two identities. First, for one slot, splitting the logical column at $h$ and using
$c+B\val z=\val{(c,z)}$,
\begin{equation}
  \pol{P^{\mathrm{log}}_c}(\zeta)=\sum_{\val z<h}\eq(\zeta,z)\,q\bigl(t+\val{(c,z)}\bigr)+p_c\bigl(1+\pol{H_h}(\zeta)\bigr)
  =\bigl\langle W^{t,e}_{u(c,\zeta)},q\bigr\rangle+p_c\bigl(1+\pol{H_h}(\zeta)\bigr),
  \label{eq:split}
\end{equation}
because $\prod_{j<b}u(c,\zeta)_j^{a_j}=[a_{<b}=c]$ selects $a=(c,z)$, the remaining factor is
$\eq(\zeta,z)$, and $t+\val a<e=t+Bh$ is exactly $\val z<h$ once $c<B$. So step~1 makes
$C_c=\inner{W^{t,e}_{u(c,\zeta_c)}}q$ for every claim.

Second, for a complete block, fix $a\in\cube{b+k}$ in~\eqref{eq:weight}. Its selector factor
under $u(c,\zeta)$ is $[a_{<b}=c]$, so summing the $B$ slots with their consecutive ranks,
\[
  \sum_{c\in\cube b}\gamma^{\beta+\val c}\prod_{j<b}u(c,\zeta)_j^{a_j}
  =\gamma^{\beta+\val{a_{<b}}}=\gamma^{\beta}\prod_{j<b}\bigl(\gamma^{2^j}\bigr)^{a_j},
\]
which is $\gamma^\beta$ times the selector factor of $u^{\star}(\gamma,\zeta)$; the row
coordinates are identical. Hence
$\sum_{c}\gamma^{\beta+\val c}W^{t,e}_{u(c,\zeta)}=\gamma^{\beta}W^{t,e}_{u^{\star}(\gamma,\zeta)}$,
and $W$ of step~3 is $\sum_c\gamma^{\mathrm{rk}(c)}W^{t,e}_{u(c,\zeta_c)}$. Completeness
follows. For the converse, $\inner Wq-C=\sum_c\gamma^{\mathrm{rk}(c)}\bigl(\inner{W^{t,e}_{u(c,\zeta_c)}}q-C_c\bigr)$
is a nonzero polynomial in $\gamma$ of degree below the number of claims.
\end{proof}

Nothing else is proved about the reduction: it is an identity, and the soundness of the
opening is that of the PCS, degraded only by the $\gamma$ term above. Two
evaluations are left for the verifier, $\pol{H_h}$ and $\pol W$.

\paragraph{The prefix indicator.} $\pol{H_h}(\zeta)$ is a two-state pass over $\cube k$, most
significant bit first, with $\mathsf{lt}$ (already smaller) and $\mathsf{tie}$ (equal so far)
starting at $(0,1)$:
\begin{equation}
  (\mathsf{lt},\mathsf{tie})\longmapsto
  \begin{cases}
    \bigl(\mathsf{lt},\ \mathsf{tie}(1+\zeta_j)\bigr) & h_j=0,\\[2pt]
    \bigl(\mathsf{lt}+\mathsf{tie}(1+\zeta_j),\ \mathsf{tie}\,\zeta_j\bigr) & h_j=1,
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:prefix}
\end{equation}
and $\pol{H_h}(\zeta)=\mathsf{lt}$ at the end ($\equiv1$ when $h=2^k$). Branch-free, for a
circuit verifier with Boolean-constrained $h_j$:
$\mathsf{lt}\mathrel{+}=h_j\mathsf{tie}(1+\zeta_j)$,
$\mathsf{tie}\mathrel{*}=1+h_j+\zeta_j$. Two multiplications per bit. The height is the
per-column row count $h$, not $Bh$. The rest of the note evaluates $\pol W$.

%=====================================================================
\section{The interval weight is a branching program}\label{sec:automaton}
%=====================================================================

Fix $t,e,u$ as in~\eqref{eq:weight} and set $u_j=(1,0)$ for $n\le j\le m$. The automaton
reads positions $j=0,\dots,m$, least significant first, with state
\[
  \st0=(0,0),\quad \st1=(1,0),\quad \st2=(0,1),\quad \st3=(1,1),\qquad\text{index }\kappa+2\lambda,
\]
where $\kappa$ is the carry of $\val w=t+\val a$ over the bits read and $\lambda$ is the
comparison $\val w<e$ over those bits (a more significant differing bit overrides a less
significant one, so reading upward each differing bit overwrites $\lambda$). Two registers,
hence width four~\cite{jagged}.

At position $j$ let $t_j,e_j$ be the bits of $t,e$, let $x_j$ be the evaluation coordinate,
and write $\chi_j^0:=1+x_j$, $\chi_j^1:=x_j$ for the two per-coordinate factors of $\eq$, so
that $\eq(u,x)=\prod_j\chi_j^{u_j}$ for Boolean $u$. Set
\[
  w_0:=u_j^0\chi_j^0,\quad w_1:=u_j^1\chi_j^0,\quad w_2:=u_j^0\chi_j^1,\quad w_3:=u_j^1\chi_j^1 .
\]
Reading a row bit $a$ and an index bit $\omega$ from $(\kappa,\lambda)$ is permitted only
when $\omega=a\oplus\kappa\oplus t_j$, and then
\[
  \kappa'=\maj(a,\kappa,t_j),\qquad
  \lambda'=\begin{cases}\lambda & \omega=e_j,\\ e_j & \omega\neq e_j,\end{cases}
  \qquad\text{with weight } u_j^a\chi_j^{\omega}.
\]
The output is the mass in $\st2$ after position $m$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:automaton}
For every $x\in\F^m$ the automaton outputs
\[
  \sum_{\substack{a\in\cube n\\ t+\val a<e}}\Bigl(\prod_{j<n}u_j^{a_j}\Bigr)\eq\bigl(t+\val a,\,x\bigr)\;=\;\pol{W^{t,e}_u}(x).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Expanding the product of transition matrices, a path is a choice of $(a_j,\omega_j)_{j\le m}$
of weight $\prod_j u_j^{a_j}\chi_j^{\omega_j}$. The constraint
$\omega_j=a_j\oplus\kappa_j\oplus t_j$ with $\kappa_{j+1}=\maj(a_j,\kappa_j,t_j)$,
$\kappa_0=0$, is binary addition, so paths biject with choices of $a$ and satisfy
$\val\omega=t+\val a$; then $\prod_j\chi_j^{\omega_j}=\eq(\omega,x)$, and $u_j=(1,0)$ for
$j\ge n$ kills every $a$ with a bit at or above $n$. Acceptance is $t+\val a<e$: $\lambda$
ends at $1$ exactly when the most significant position where $\omega$ and $e$ differ has
$e$-bit $1$, and $\kappa$ ends at $0$ exactly when the addition does not carry out of
position $m$. The extra position $j=m$, where $u_m=(1,0)$ and $x_m:=0$ force
$a_m=\omega_m=0$, supplies that carry test and also represents $e=2^m$ (there $e_m=1$, which
sets $\lambda$ to $1$). Both sides are multilinear in every coordinate of $x$ and agree at
Boolean arguments, hence agree everywhere.
\end{proof}

Since $t_j,e_j$ are public bits, the step selects one of four fixed matrices instead of
evaluating a tensor over $(a,\omega)$. With $\st{}=(\st0,\st1,\st2,\st3)$ incoming and
$\st{}'$ outgoing:
\[
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.25}
\begin{array}{l@{\qquad}l}
(t_j,e_j)=(0,0): &
\begin{aligned}
\st0'&=\st0(w_0+w_3)+(\st1+\st3)w_2+\st2w_3, & \st1'&=\st1w_1,\\
\st2'&=\st2w_0, & \st3'&=\st3w_1,
\end{aligned}\\[2.2ex]
(t_j,e_j)=(0,1): &
\begin{aligned}
\st0'&=\st0w_3+\st1w_2, & \st1'&=0,\\
\st2'&=\st0w_0+\st2(w_0+w_3)+\st3w_2, & \st3'&=(\st1+\st3)w_1,
\end{aligned}\\[2.2ex]
(t_j,e_j)=(1,0): &
\begin{aligned}
\st0'&=(\st0+\st2)w_2, & \st1'&=\st0w_1+\st1(w_0+w_3)+\st3w_3,\\
\st2'&=0, & \st3'&=\st2w_1+\st3w_0,
\end{aligned}\\[2.2ex]
(t_j,e_j)=(1,1): &
\begin{aligned}
\st0'&=\st0w_2, & \st1'&=\st1w_3,\\
\st2'&=\st2w_2, & \st3'&=(\st0+\st2)w_1+\st1w_0+\st3(w_0+w_3).
\end{aligned}
\end{array}
\]
Each arm costs $6$ multiplications. The $w_i$ cost one more: for a row pair $(1+r,r)$ form
$\pi=rx$ and read off $w_0=1+r+x+\pi$, $w_1=r+\pi$, $w_2=x+\pi$, $w_3=\pi$; for a selector
pair $(1,\gamma^{2^j})$ form $\pi=\gamma^{2^j}x$ and read off $w_0=1+x$,
$w_1=\gamma^{2^j}+\pi$, $w_2=x$, $w_3=\pi$, the successive $\gamma^{2^j}$ coming from
repeated squaring. A position costs $7$ multiplications, a run $7(m+1)$. One run per
\emph{block}, not per column: that is what step~2 of Protocol~\ref{prot:jagged} buys, and it
is why~\eqref{eq:weight} was stated for weights that need not sum to $1$. Normalising
$u^\star$, that is factoring $(1,\gamma^{2^j})=(1+\gamma^{2^j})\cdot(1,\gamma^{2^j}/(1+\gamma^{2^j}))$,
would cost $b$ inversions and fail at $\gamma=1$.

\section{What the verifier evaluates}\label{sec:contract}
%=====================================================================

A BaseFold/WHIR-style opening never evaluates $\pol W$ at a point. It consumes $m-L$ of the
$m$ variables in sumcheck rounds, leaving a challenge prefix $\rho\in\F^{m-L}$, and
transmits the remaining $L$-variable message $\mathrm{yr}:\cube L\to\F$ in the clear, $L$
being small (typically $3$ or $4$). Its last check needs
\begin{equation}
  \Sigma:=\sum_{y\in\cube L}\mathrm{yr}(y)\,\pol W(\rho,y).
  \label{eq:contract}
\end{equation}
Running the automaton $2^L$ times costs $2^L\cdot7(m+1)$ per block. One run suffices.

\paragraph{Matrix form.} Let $T_j(x_j)\in\F^{4\times4}$ be the transitions above. Then
\[
  \pol W(x)=\mathbf e_{\st0}^{\!\top}\Bigl(\prod_{j=0}^{m}T_j(x_j)\Bigr)\mathbf e_{\st2}
          =\mathbf e_{\st0}^{\!\top}\Bigl(\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}T_j(x_j)\Bigr)\mathbf u,
  \qquad \mathbf u:=T_m(0)\,\mathbf e_{\st2},
\]
and reading the transition rule at $j=m$ (where $a_m=\omega_m=0$, $t_m=0$) gives
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf u=(\,e_m,\ 0,\ 1,\ 0\,),
  \label{eq:accept}
\end{equation}
so $\mathbf u=\mathbf e_{\st2}$ unless the block ends exactly at $e=2^m$. Splitting the rest
at $j=m-L$ and setting $S:=\mathbf e_{\st0}^{\!\top}\prod_{j<m-L}T_j(\rho_j)\in\F^4$,
\begin{equation}
  \Sigma=\inner SV,\qquad
  V:=\sum_{y\in\cube L}\mathrm{yr}(y)\Bigl(\prod_{j=m-L}^{m-1}T_j\bigl(y_{j-(m-L)}\bigr)\Bigr)\mathbf u .
  \label{eq:SV}
\end{equation}
$S$ is one automaton run of $m-L$ steps. $V$ is a reverse dynamic program: the residual
index coordinates are Boolean, so $T_j$ collapses to $T_j^{(0)}$ or $T_j^{(1)}$ and
\[
  V_j(y')=T_j^{(0)}V_{j+1}(y',0)+T_j^{(1)}V_{j+1}(y',1),\qquad V_m(y)=\mathrm{yr}(y)\,\mathbf u,
\]
peeling the most significant residual bit first: $2^L-1$ reverse steps, then $\inner SV$.

\paragraph{The reverse step.} It takes the child vectors $v$ (index bit $0$) and $w$ (index
bit $1$), the row coordinate $r$, and $t_j,e_j$. Transposing the transitions with row pair
$(1+r,r)$ gives, for $(t_j,e_j)=(0,0)$,
\[
  V'=\bigl((1+r)v_0+rw_0,\ \ rv_1+(1+r)w_0,\ \ (1+r)v_2+rw_0,\ \ rv_3+(1+r)w_0\bigr),
\]
and analogously for the other three: $8$ multiplications. For $r\in\{0,1\}$ the step is a
pure selection and costs nothing: $(v_0,w_0,v_2,w_0)$ at $r=0$, $(w_0,v_1,w_0,v_3)$ at
$r=1$. The row point is zero above a column's logical dimension, so the free branch fires on
every residual coordinate the column does not use.

\paragraph{Closed form when all residual row coordinates vanish.} With the high row bits
pinned to zero, $\val w=t+\val a$ forces $w$'s high part to be
$t_{\mathrm{hi}}:=\lfloor t/2^{m-L}\rfloor$ plus the incoming carry, so each state reaches
exactly one message index. With $e_{\mathrm{hi}}:=\lfloor e/2^{m-L}\rfloor$ read off the
$L+1$ top bits of $e$ (the extra bit covering $e=2^m$),
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \Sigma=\ &\mathrm{yr}(t_{\mathrm{hi}})\Bigl(S_{\st0}[\,t_{\mathrm{hi}}<e_{\mathrm{hi}}\,]+S_{\st2}[\,t_{\mathrm{hi}}\le e_{\mathrm{hi}}\,]\Bigr)\\
  +\ &\mathrm{yr}(t_{\mathrm{hi}}{+}1)\Bigl(S_{\st1}[\,t_{\mathrm{hi}}{+}1<e_{\mathrm{hi}}\,]+S_{\st3}[\,t_{\mathrm{hi}}{+}1\le e_{\mathrm{hi}}\,]\Bigr).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:closed}
\end{equation}
The high part decides $\val w<e$ when it differs from $e_{\mathrm{hi}}$; when it agrees the
prefix comparison decides, so $\st0$ (with $\lambda=0$) contributes only under a strict $<$
while $\st2$ (with $\lambda=1$) also contributes at equality, and likewise for $\st1,\st3$
at $t_{\mathrm{hi}}+1$. Cost $O(L)$: a ripple increment of $t_{\mathrm{hi}}$, one MSB-first
pass yielding $[\,t_{\mathrm{hi}}<e_{\mathrm{hi}}\,]$, $[\,t_{\mathrm{hi}}=e_{\mathrm{hi}}\,]$
and $[\,e_{\mathrm{hi}}=t_{\mathrm{hi}}{+}1\,]$ (the last converting $<$ into
$t_{\mathrm{hi}}{+}1<e_{\mathrm{hi}}$ via
$[\,t_{\mathrm{hi}}<e_{\mathrm{hi}}\,]\cdot(1+[\,e_{\mathrm{hi}}=t_{\mathrm{hi}}{+}1\,])$),
and two message reads. Address $\mathrm{yr}$ with one zero sentinel after its $2^L$ entries
so that a carry out of the last index reads in range.

\begin{center}
\fbox{\parbox{0.93\textwidth}{
\textbf{Terminal evaluation of one block's weight.}
\emph{Input:} bits of $t,e$; width $b$; scalar $\gamma$ and base rank $\beta$; row point
$\zeta$, zero-padded; round challenges $\rho\in\F^{m-L}$; final message $\mathrm{yr}$.
\emph{Output:} the block's term of~\eqref{eq:contract}.
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1pt]
\item Run the automaton over $j=0,\dots,m-L-1$ at coordinates $\rho_j$ with the weights
$u^{\star}(\gamma,\zeta)$ of~\eqref{eq:ustar}, giving $S\in\F^4$. Cost $7(m-L)$.
\item If $\zeta$ vanishes at every residual coordinate, return
$\gamma^{\beta}\cdot$\eqref{eq:closed}. Cost $O(L)$.
\item Otherwise set $V(y)=\mathrm{yr}(y)\mathbf u$ for $y\in\cube L$, apply one reverse step
to each pair for $j=m-1$ down to $m-L$, and return $\gamma^{\beta}\inner SV$. Cost at most
$8(2^L-1)+4$, and zero per step whose row coordinate is Boolean.
\end{enumerate}
}}
\end{center}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:topbit}
Step~3 is exact with $\mathbf u=\mathbf e_{\st2}$ only when the block does not end at $2^m$;
otherwise~\eqref{eq:accept} must be used. Step~2 consults that bit through
$e_{\mathrm{hi}}$, so the closed form is unconditional.
\end{remark}

%=====================================================================
\section{Cost}\label{sec:cost}
%=====================================================================

\emph{Prover.} One commitment to $q$, of length $2^m$ within a factor of two of the real
area, and no second oracle. The only work specific to the adapter is building $W$ over
$\cube m$: one $\eq$ table per distinct opening point, then one multiply-accumulate per
(cell, claim) pair into that claim's interval.

\emph{Verifier.} Per block, $7(m-L)$ multiplications for the automaton prefix, at most
$8(2^L-1)+4$ for the contraction, and $2k$ for~\eqref{eq:prefix}, so roughly $7m$
multiplications per \emph{block} rather than per column. The
computation is straight-line in $m$ and the block count, with the shape of the data entering
only as bits, which is what makes it cheap to prove recursively.
