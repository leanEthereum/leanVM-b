\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This note describes the two Jagged constructions used in leanVM. Unequal-height trace columns are packed into one Ligerito commitment without a power-of-two pad after every column. Independently, the grand-product leaves concatenate the real rows of all bus blocks and pad only once. Two quadratic sumchecks reduce the tight grand product to column evaluations at one common point, and a third sumcheck folds those evaluations together with all local constraints. The grand-product construction and its common-point reduction are not part of the original Jagged PCS paper~\cite{Jagged26}.
\end{abstract}

\section{The interval-transport polynomial}

All bit vectors are little-endian. For \(x,u\in\F^m\), define
\[
\eq(x,u):=\prod_{i=0}^{m-1}(1+x_i+u_i).
\]
On the Boolean cube this is the equality indicator, so the multilinear extension of \(P:\cube m\to\F\) is \(\mle P(x)=\sum_{u\in\cube m}P(u)\eq(x,u)\).

Suppose a logical table starts at dense offset \(t\) and has \(h\) real entries. Its fundamental weight is
\[
J_{t,h}(r,x):=\sum_{u=0}^{h-1}\eq(r,u)\eq(x,t+u).
\]
At Boolean \(x\), this is zero outside \([t,t+h)\), while \(J_{t,h}(r,t+u)=\eq(r,u)\). Consequently, if \(q[t+u]=P[u]\) for \(0\leq u<h\), then
\[
\sum_{v\in\cube m}q[v]J_{t,h}(r,v)=\sum_{u=0}^{h-1}P[u]\eq(r,u).
\]
This identity is the common core of both constructions below.

\paragraph{Width-four evaluation.} The verifier evaluates \(J\) in \(O(m)\) field operations. Scan bits from least to most significant and maintain four states \((\kappa,\chi)\in\{0,1\}^2\). Here \(\kappa\) is the carry in \(x=t+u\), and \(\chi\) records whether the processed prefix of \(u\), read with the current bit as the most significant one seen so far, is strictly smaller than the corresponding prefix of \(h\). For Boolean bits \(a=u_i\), \(d=x_i\), \(s=t_i\), and \(\ell=h_i\),
\[
d=a+s+\kappa\pmod 2,\qquad \kappa'=\left\lfloor\frac{a+s+\kappa}{2}\right\rfloor,\qquad
\chi'=\begin{cases}\chi,&a=\ell,\\ \ell,&a\neq\ell.\end{cases}
\]
Start in \((0,0)\), process one additional zero bit above the dense cube, and accept \((0,1)\). The extra bit supports \(t+h=2^m\) and rejects overflow. At coordinate \(i\), the two choices of \(a\) have weights \((1+r_i,r_i)\), and the two choices of \(d\) have weights \((1+x_i,x_i)\). Summing the valid weighted transitions gives the next four field elements. This is the Basic Jagged read-once branching program~\cite{Jagged26}.

The same evaluator also accepts field points \(\sigma,\lambda\) in place of the Boolean bit strings \(\bits(t),\bits(h)\). At each coordinate, compute the transition for the four Boolean choices \((s,\ell)\in\cube2\), then multilinearly interpolate those four results at \((\sigma_i,\lambda_i)\). We denote the result by
\[
J(r,x;\sigma,\lambda).
\]
For Boolean parameter points it equals \(J_{t,h}(r,x)\); for arbitrary points it is the multilinear extension of that function in all start and length bits.

\section{Jagged trace commitment}

\paragraph{Shifted prefixes.} A logical column \(P_i\) has \(2^{k_i}\) entries, of which the first \(h_i\leq2^{k_i}\) are witness values and the remaining entries equal a public padding value \(p_i\). Define
\[
Q_i[z]=\begin{cases}P_i[z]+p_i,&z<h_i,\\0,&h_i\leq z<2^{k_i}.\end{cases}
\]
Then \(P_i=Q_i+p_i\) on the whole Boolean cube and therefore
\[
\mle P_i(r)=\mle Q_i(r)+p_i.
\]
Committing \(Q_i\), rather than the unshifted real prefix, removes every padding-prefix correction from the verifier.

\paragraph{Layout.} Compatible shifted prefixes of equal height are grouped into row-major blocks of power-of-two width \(B=2^b\). If a block starts at \(t\), then
\[
q[t+c+Bz]=Q_c[z],\qquad 0\leq c<B,\quad 0\leq z<h.
\]
The block occupies exactly \(Bh\) dense cells. Wider blocks are placed first, which aligns every block to its width without inserting gaps. Flock's packed witness \(q_{\rm pkd}\) remains an unshifted, full power-of-two block anchored at zero; all ordinary columns use shifted real prefixes. After the last block, \(q\) is padded once with zero to a cube of size \(2^M\). The dimension \(M\) is large enough to hold the packed area, every block selector followed by its longest row point, and the PCS minimum domain.

\paragraph{Opening one column.} For slot \(c\) of a width-\(2^b\) block, form
\[
r^\star=(\bits_b(c),r_0,\ldots,r_{k-1},0,\ldots,0).
\]
The selector bits choose the slot and the row bits choose the logical entry. With \(L=2^bh\),
\[
\sum_x q[x]J_{t,L}(r^\star,x)=\sum_{z=0}^{h-1}Q_c[z]\eq(r,z)=\mle Q_c(r).
\]
The arithmetization recovers the full logical evaluation by adding \(p_c\).

\paragraph{Batching a row-major block.} Suppose the \(B\) slots have batching coefficients \(1,\gamma,\ldots,\gamma^{B-1}\). Instead of evaluating one \(J\) per slot, replace the selector-coordinate pairs \((1+r_i,r_i)\) by
\[
(1,\gamma^{2^i}),\qquad 0\leq i<b.
\]
For slot \(c=\sum_i2^ic_i\), their product is \(\gamma^c\). One width-four evaluation therefore returns the complete geometric batch without an inversion or normalization. The prover writes the corresponding dense weight vector and invokes the ordinary Ligerito opening; Ligerito itself is unchanged.

\paragraph{Recursive Ligerito terminal.} After Ligerito has folded the first \(f\) dense coordinates, let its final message be \(m:\cube\ell\to\F\), where \(f+\ell=M\). The verifier first applies the four-state transition through the fixed coordinates. It then contracts the \(2^\ell\) final-message entries in reverse through the two Boolean transition matrices, costing \(O(4\cdot2^\ell)\) per block. When every residual logical-row coordinate is fixed to zero, the surviving state identifies at most two adjacent entries of \(m\); the implementation uses this closed form instead of traversing the whole residual tree.

\section{A tight grand product}

\subsection{Leaves and the first reduction}

A bus block \(b\) has \(h_b\) real rows and tuple coordinates \(c_{b,j}(u)\). Its fingerprint is
\[
\phi_b(u)=\gamma+\sum_j\alpha^j c_{b,j}(u),\qquad 0\leq u<h_b.
\]
For each side, blocks are concatenated in their fixed declaration order:
\[
L_b[t_b+u]=\phi_b(u),\qquad t_0=0,\qquad t_{b+1}=t_b+h_b.
\]
Only the suffix after the final real row is padded, with the product identity \(1\). Push and pull have paired blocks with equal heights, hence identical offsets, and their product roots are compared directly. The count side is padded with \(1\) to the same depth, so one batched product GKR produces terminal claims \(v_s=\mle L_s(\zeta)\) at one common point \(\zeta\).

Because \(\sum_x\eq(\zeta,x)=1\) and every final padding leaf is \(1\),
\[
v_s+1=\sum_b\sum_{u<h_b}\bigl(\phi_b(u)+1\bigr)\eq(\zeta,t_b+u).
\]
Sample \(\eta\) and combine the push, pull, and count identities with coefficients \(1,\eta,\eta^2\). Paired push and pull blocks share one interval. For every resulting interval \(p=(t_p,h_p)\), collect its constants, index coordinate, public bytecode coordinates, and committed coordinates into one local multilinear \(A_p\). The combined claim is
\[
C=\sum_p\sum_{u<h_p}A_p(u)\eq(\zeta,t_p+u).
\]

Let \(\nu\) be the largest real-row dimension and, for an ordinary committed source, the largest logical column dimension:
\[
\nu=\max\left(\max_p\left\lceil\log_2\max(h_p,1)\right\rceil,\ \max_{i\ {\rm committed}} k_i\right).
\]
Define \(W_p(u)=\mathbf 1[u<h_p]\eq(\zeta,t_p+u)\) on \(\cube\nu\). A degree-two sumcheck reduces
\[
C=\sum_{u\in\cube\nu}\sum_p \mle A_p(u)\mle W_p(u)
\]
to a random row point \(\rho\), giving
\[
C_\rho=\sum_p a_pJ_{t_p,h_p}(\rho,\zeta),\qquad a_p:=\mle A_p(\rho).
\]
Each \(a_p\) is a public linear combination of ordinary source evaluations. Let \(\nu_i=\lceil\log_2\max(h_i,1)\rceil\). For a committed shifted prefix, the prover exposes the common-point value
\[
e_i=\mle Q_i(\rho_0,\ldots,\rho_{\nu_i-1})\prod_{j=\nu_i}^{\nu-1}(1+\rho_j).
\]
The verifier checks the inverse of the displayed tail product and recovers the source value used in \(a_p\) as
\[
\mle P_i(\rho_0,\ldots,\rho_{\nu_i-1},0,\ldots,0)=
\frac{e_i}{\prod_{j=\nu_i}^{\nu-1}(1+\rho_j)}+p_i.
\]
Thus every ordinary committed bus source is already claimed at the same \(\nu\)-coordinate point \(\rho\). Virtual \(q_{\rm pkd}\) slot claims remain at their native instance point. The index coordinate \(u\mapsto g^u\) is evaluated directly as
\[
\prod_{i<\nu_p}\bigl(1+\rho_i+g^{2^i}\rho_i\bigr).
\]
Thus the only nonstandard terminal quantity is the sum of many Jagged weights in \(C_\rho\).

\subsection{The start--length assist}

Evaluating one width-four automaton per interval would make recursive verification expensive. Instead define two tables over Boolean start and length strings:
\[
S(s,\ell):=\sum_{p:\,(\bits(t_p),\bits(h_p))=(s,\ell)}a_p,\qquad
K(s,\ell):=J_{t,h}(\rho,\zeta)\quad\text{when }(s,\ell)=(\bits(t),\bits(h)).
\]
Then
\[
C_\rho=\sum_{s,\ell}S(s,\ell)K(s,\ell).
\]
A second degree-two sumcheck runs over the start bits followed by the length bits. At its random terminal point \((\sigma,\lambda)\), the verifier checks
\[
\widetilde S(\sigma,\lambda)=\sum_p a_p\eq(\sigma,\bits(t_p))\eq(\lambda,\bits(h_p))
\]
and
\[
\widetilde K(\sigma,\lambda)=J(\rho,\zeta;\sigma,\lambda).
\]
The first expression is a sparse-table evaluation; the second is one generalized width-four evaluation. Therefore the verifier performs one Jagged automaton for the entire grand product, rather than one per block.

\paragraph{Recursive evaluation of \(\widetilde S\).} Every height bit is derived from a value already certified by recursion: a table row count, the used-memory prefix, the program-bound bytecode prefix, or a structural power of two. Blocks with the same symbolic height share the length equality product. Starts are not hints: in the fixed block order, a binary prefix adder derives \(t_{b+1}=t_b+h_b\). The same pass multiplies \(\eq(\sigma,\bits(t_b))\), so offset certification and start evaluation share all bit reads. Finally the guest forms the sparse sum above and multiplies it by the single value \(J(\rho,\zeta;\sigma,\lambda)\). For the main benchmark shape, the first sumcheck has \(20\) rounds and the assist has \(23+21\) rounds; all fixed-count loops in these small kernels are unrolled.

\paragraph{Prover evaluation.} The first sumcheck stores each interval only at its local size \(2^{\nu_p}\). Once all local coordinates have been folded, the remaining row bits are forced to zero by \(W_p\), so the prover updates the interval's scalar without allocating a \(2^\nu\)-entry table. The assist sumcheck evaluates the sparse interval list directly. Its terminal computation is linear in the number of blocks and uses one width-four automaton.

\section{One common opening point}

The tight reduction above produces ordinary bus claims \(e_j=\mle Q_j(\rho)\) at one point but would still leave the six instruction-table constraints at separate points. We follow the common-point opening reduction used by SP1~\cite{SP1} and fold the bus claims into one global constraint sumcheck.

For each table \(t\), let \(C_t(\eta_t;\cdot)\) be the random linear combination of its degree-two row constraints, and sample a batching challenge \(\theta\). Every shifted column is zero-extended to the common \(\nu\)-cube. Define
\[
F(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{B-1}\theta^j Q_j(x)+\sum_{t=0}^{5}\theta^{B+t}
C_t\bigl(\eta_t;(Q_c(x)+p_c)_{c\in I_t}\bigr),
\]
where the first sum follows the canonical order of the \(B\) ordinary bus claims and \(I_t\) is the constraint-column list of table \(t\). At Boolean rows inside a table, \(Q_c+p_c=P_c\); on its padding suffix and outside its native cube, \(Q_c=0\), so \(Q_c+p_c=p_c\), which is the valid public padding row. Hence every \(C_t\) vanishes on the common Boolean cube and
\[
\sum_{x\in\cube\nu}\eq(\rho,x)F(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{B-1}\theta^j e_j.
\]
A degree-two sumcheck reduces this identity to a new point \(\omega\). The common \(\eq(\rho,x)\) factor is kept outside the product polynomial, so each round still sends only its values at \(0,1,g\). At the terminal, the prover sends each distinct \(\mle Q_c(\omega)\); the verifier evaluates \(F(\omega)\), and the Jagged PCS opens every ordinary bus and constraint column at \(\omega\). Only the strided \(q_{\rm pkd}\) claims and the public-input memory claim use other points.

This fusion has two effects in recursion: six independent constraint sumchecks become one, and columns that previously belonged to many row-point groups now share complete row-major Jagged blocks. Committing shifted prefixes additionally makes every terminal claim equal to the committed value, so no prefix-indicator evaluation remains.

\section{Soundness and measured cost}

The transcript absorbs the GKR terminal claims before sampling the challenges that batch them. The first sumcheck proves the reduction from the complete GKR leaf claims to normalized source evaluations. The start--length assist proves the remaining sparse Jagged-weight identity; its terminal start and length bits are bound to certified public sizes and canonical prefix sums, so no interval boundary is accepted as advice. The global sumcheck is bound to the bus claims before \(\theta\) and its round challenges are sampled. Its random linear combination contributes at most the number of batched identities divided by \(|\F|\) to the soundness error. A zero tail product in the common-point normalization is rejected. The implementation reserves one additional grinding bit for the batching and reduction checks.

On the standard command
\[
\texttt{RAYON\_NUM\_THREADS=11 cargo run --release -- recursion --n 2},
\]
two \(975{,}099\)-cycle inner proofs produce a \(1{,}772{,}858\)-cycle recursion guest. This is \(74{,}082\) cycles (\(4.01\%\)) below the first tight-grand-product version and \(92{,}460\) cycles (\(4.96\%\)) below the former internally padded grand product. The recursion program contains \(530{,}378\) instructions and pads to \(2^{20}\). A representative run proves in \(6.44\) seconds and verifies in \(0.56\) seconds; proof size varies slightly with Merkle-query collisions and is about \(596\)--\(598\) KiB.

Tests cover exhaustive small intervals, arbitrary row-weight pairs, exact-full endpoints, Boolean start--length agreement with dense MLEs, native proof round trips, six runtime shapes verified by the same compiled recursion guest, mixed-shape recursion, and adversarial mutations of the named hints.
