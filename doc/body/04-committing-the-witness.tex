\section{Committing the witness}\label{sec:rs}

\subsection{Stacking}\label{sec:stacking}

Suppose we want to commit to $T$ multilinears $P_1,\dots,P_T$ over boolean hypercubes of various sizes. We could commit to each independently, at the cost of a large cumulative opening proof. Instead they are concatenated into a large multilinear polynomial $q$, which we commit to with an inner-product commitment scheme (Definition~\ref{def:ipcs}), with a protocol to reduce opening claims on $P_i$ to a claim on $q$. Order them by size, $\kappa_1\ge\dots\ge\kappa_T$ variables, and concatenate their tables end to end,
\[
  q \;=\; P_1 \,\Vert\, P_2 \,\Vert\,\dots\,\Vert\, P_T \,\Vert\, 0,
\]
zero-padded to length $2^{M}$, with $M=\bigl\lceil\log_2\textstyle\sum_i 2^{\kappa_i}\bigr\rceil$. Placed largest first, column $i$ starts at an offset that is a multiple of $2^{\kappa_i}$; writing $\mathsf{sel}_i\in\cube{M-\kappa_i}$ for the high bits of that offset,
\[
  \widetilde P_i(z)\;=\;\widetilde q(z,\mathsf{sel}_i)\qquad(z\in\cube{\kappa_i}).
\]
An evaluation claim $\widetilde P_i(\zeta)=c$ is therefore a claim on the stack,
\[
  \widetilde q(\zeta,\mathsf{sel}_i)\;=\;\sum_{w\in\cube{M}}\eq\bigl((\zeta,\mathsf{sel}_i),w\bigr)\,\widetilde q(w)\;=\;c,
\]
a weighted sum over $\widetilde q$ with weight $W(w)=\eq((\zeta,\mathsf{sel}_i),w)$.

\paragraph{Batching.} Every claim the protocol raises against a column is a weighted sum $\langle W_j,\widetilde q\rangle=c_j$ with $\E$-valued weight and target. After the claimed values are bound, the verifier samples independent $\lambda_j\in\E$ and defines $W_\lambda=\sum_j\lambda_jW_j$ and $C_\lambda=\sum_j\lambda_jc_j$. The inner-product PCS (Annex~\ref{annex:pcs}) proves
\[
  \sum_{w\in\cube{M}} W_\lambda(w)\,\widetilde q(w)=C_\lambda
\]

\subsection{Packing the Boolean witness}\label{sec:ringswitch}

Flock's BLAKE3 argument (\S\ref{sec:tab-blake3}) produces claims about Boolean multilinears. The VM packs them into $\K$-words for commitment and opens the resulting claims over $\E$, the specialization
\[
  \Ftwo \subset \K \subset \E
\]
of ring switching~\cite{DP26}. Annex~\ref{annex:rs} is the construction and its soundness; this section is what flock hands over and where it lands in the stack.

\paragraph{Packing.} Let $\npkd$ be the number of Boolean variables left after the six that select a bit within a $64$-bit word, so the Boolean witness is $64$ multilinears $Q_0,\dots,Q_{63}:\cube{\npkd}\to\Ftwo$, that is $\npkd+6$ Boolean variables and $64\cdot2^{\npkd}$ values in all. In the polynomial basis $1,x,\dots,x^{63}$ of $\K/\Ftwo$, pack each word as
\[
  Q_\pkd(u)=\sum_{i=0}^{63}Q_i(u)\,x^i\in\K,
  \qquad u\in\cube{\npkd}.
\]
The PCS commits the $2^{\npkd}$ values of $Q_\pkd$, stored in the $q_\pkd$ region of the witness stack (\S\ref{sec:stacking}). This represents the Boolean witness with no embedding overhead.

\paragraph{Incoming claim.} Flock supplies $c\in\E$, a point $z\in\E^{\npkd}$, and verifier-known weights $\lag_i\in\E$ for $0\le i<64$. A valid witness satisfies
\begin{equation}
  c=\sum_{i=0}^{63}\lag_i s_i,
  \qquad
  s_i=\sum_{u\in\cube{\npkd}}\eq(z,u)\,Q_i(u).
  \label{eq:rs-incoming}
\end{equation}
The $\lag_i$ are the Lagrange weights of flock's univariate skip. The prover sends $s_0,\dots,s_{63}\in\E$, and the verifier checks the first equality. It remains to bind these values to the committed $Q_\pkd$, which is exactly the $64$ claims at one shared point that Annex~\ref{annex:rs} reduces.

\paragraph{Coordinate transpose.} Take the basis $e_{64j+i}=x^iy^j$ of $\E/\Ftwo$ of \S\ref{rs:setting}, $y$ generating the cubic extension $\E/\K$. Write
\[
  s_i=\sum_{w=0}^{191}\fcoord_{w,i}\,e_w,
  \qquad \fcoord_{w,i}\in\Ftwo,
  \qquad
  t_w=\sum_{i=0}^{63}\fcoord_{w,i}\,x^i\in\K.
\]
The map $(s_i)_{i<64}\mapsto(t_w)_{w<192}$ only transposes a $192\times64$ bit matrix. Likewise, write
\[
  \eq(z,u)=\sum_{w=0}^{191}\fcoord_w(u)\,e_w,
  \qquad \fcoord_w(u)\in\Ftwo.
\]
Taking coordinates in the definition of $s_i$ gives
\begin{equation}
  t_w
  =\sum_{i=0}^{63}x^i\sum_{u\in\cube{\npkd}}\fcoord_w(u)\,Q_i(u)
  =\sum_{u\in\cube{\npkd}}\fcoord_w(u)\,Q_\pkd(u),
  \qquad 0\le w<192.
  \label{eq:rs-coordinate-claim}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Batching.} After absorbing all $s_i$, the verifier draws $f_0,\dots,f_5\leftarrow\E$ and forms the $\Ftwo$-linear map $\Phi$ of \eqref{eq:phi}, then the coefficients $\lambda_w=\Phi(e_w)$. Multiplying \eqref{eq:rs-coordinate-claim} by $\lambda_w$ and summing gives
\begin{equation}
  T:=\sum_{w=0}^{191}\lambda_w t_w
  =\sum_{u\in\cube{\npkd}}\underbrace{\Phi\bigl(\eq(z,u)\bigr)}_{W_z(u)}Q_\pkd(u).
  \label{eq:rs-batched}
\end{equation}
This is directly a weighted opening of the committed $Q_\pkd$: the target is $T\in\E$, and $W_z$ is a transparent $\E$-valued weight. No ring-switch sumcheck is needed. Multiple flock claims share the same map; independent $\E$-scalars then fold their targets and weights into the global stacked opening (\S\ref{sec:stacking}).

\paragraph{Soundness and cost.} Drawing the challenges only after the $s_i$ are fixed bounds the batching error by less than $2^{-160}$, before the PCS decoder's list-size factor (\S\ref{rs:map}). At a PCS query point $r\in\E^{\npkd}$ the verifier needs $\mle{W_z}(r)$, which the boxed formula of \S\ref{rs:weight} evaluates in $O(64\,\npkd)$ field operations, without constructing the $2^{\npkd}$-entry table; the $64$ coefficients $c_k$ of $\Phi$ come once from the same six-stage recurrence (\S\ref{rs:cost}).
