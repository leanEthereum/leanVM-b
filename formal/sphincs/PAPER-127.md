# Continuing from 126 to 127 bits

The 126-bit theorem is proved. The 127-bit bound is proved for every original hash budget $q\ge3\cdot2^{114}$ by [security127_of_large_budget](SphincsSecurity/Proof/Security127LargeBudget.lean). The full 127-bit theorem remains unfinished because smaller positive budgets still need their bound. Keep the original scheme, SUF game, signing cap and whole-experiment hash budget in [Statement.lean](SphincsSecurity/Statement.lean) fixed.

The completed large-budget reduction uses `forgeAdvantage_le_native_bound`: for $x=q/2^{128}$ and $q\le2^{127}$, original SUF probability is at most $2x-x^2+(11/65536)x+q/2^{169}+2^{-700}$. Exact Lean arithmetic bounds this by $q/2^{127}$ when $q\ge3\cdot2^{114}$; larger budgets use probability at most one. The reference dummy is explicitly instantiated by a proved valid encoding, so `security127_of_large_budget` has only the original hash-bound premise and the budget-range condition.

The native bookkeeping invariants, whole-run exception classification and both exception histories are bounded. `exceptionHistorySourceGame_prefix_le` gives $2^{-700}$. `exceptionHistorySourceGame_cache_le` gives $q/2^{169}$ by freezing a second-moment weight at the first exception, accumulating native message charges and bounding them by actual hash calls. Both histories include failed signing invocations and steps after the certificate monitor stops. Erasing their passive flags recovers the monitored source game exactly.

Its verdict, frontier and full external/verifier trace have exactly the same joint law as the corresponding projection of the previous graph-context source, so both original SUF success probability and the primitive-event probability are preserved. The witness classification is factored through an explicit retained run, and its FTS event keeps the valid-signing-log condition. Thus original SUF probability is at most $\Pr_R[E_{\rm primitive}]+\Pr_R[E_{\rm FTS}]$, where the latter event contains the full certificate, near certificate with an undisclosed true-secret query, or two distinct undisclosed true-secret queries for the same retained forgery and signing history. The certificate probability transfer is proved below; the two remaining FTS alternatives still need their estimates.

The certificate probability transfer is proved in [ReferenceCertificateCoverage.lean](SphincsSecurity/Proof/ReferenceCertificateCoverage.lean). [ReferenceCertificateTrace.lean](SphincsSecurity/Proof/ReferenceCertificateTrace.lean) identifies the joint law of the retained key, forgery, signing log, verification result and message-query record with the original random-oracle execution. Successful signing entries and the forgery's verification supply all message rows needed by the transcript certificate. Their outputs agree with the fixed oracle, and [BoundaryCertificateCache.lean](SphincsSecurity/Proof/BoundaryCertificateCache.lean) embeds the recorded rows in the original final cache. Certificate coverage is preserved under this embedding, with own-input exclusion unchanged. Thus the retained full-certificate probability is at most the already bounded original full-certificate probability.

Consequently `forgeAdvantage_le_remainingFts_small_budget` proves $\Pr[\mathrm{SUF}]\le(7/4+\delta)x+2^{-700}+\Pr_R[E_{\rm remainingFTS}]$ for $q\le3\cdot2^{114}$ from the original hash bound. The remaining event is exactly the near-certificate/undisclosed-guess or two-distinct-undisclosed-guesses alternative on the actual retained forgery and valid signing log. The next work is to prove those two probability estimates, then combine the resulting small-budget numerical bound with the completed large-budget theorem and audit the public 127-bit theorem.

The continuing hidden-secret observation law is proved in [SecretGuessObservation.lean](SphincsSecurity/Proof/SecretGuessObservation.lean). Unlike the older first-hit stop, a true guess retires its coordinate and execution continues. The exact posterior retains the secret table, result, remaining candidates, retired coordinates and distinct-guess set. Every active coordinate has at least $N-q$ candidates after at most $q$ probes, giving the conditional true-guess rate at most $(N-q)^{-1}$. Disclosures retire coordinates without increasing the guess count. [FtsGuessSigning.lean](SphincsSecurity/Proof/FtsGuessSigning.lean) identifies the original key-generation secret prior, reconstructs the original signing result and boundary trace through the disclosure interpreter, and proves the signing posterior and unchanged guess/probe counters. The external-query translation, adaptive execution correspondence and original-budget transfer are proved below. The witness-to-guess implications and forced-law message/certificate estimates still need to be connected before this law supplies either remaining FTS probability bound.

[FtsGuessHash.lean](SphincsSecurity/Proof/FtsGuessHash.lean) translates every external hash query into a secret test or a secret-independent auxiliary lookup, preserving the full reply even when a missed test returns the canonical output by coincidence. [FtsGuessProgram.lean](SphincsSecurity/Proof/FtsGuessProgram.lean) extends the translation through adaptive signing and final verification, retaining the forgery, signing log, boundary work and external/verifier hash traces. [FtsGuessReference.lean](SphincsSecurity/Proof/FtsGuessReference.lean) specializes the signer to the existing reference experiment, proves that its root and auxiliary handlers are independent of the FTS secrets, and connects the original FTS secret prior to the continuing lazy interpreter. The exact fixed-secret law includes the completed verifier record. These correspondences do not yet bound the two remaining FTS events: the tracked guesses must still be related to their witnesses and combined with the required certificate estimates.

[FtsGuessWork.lean](SphincsSecurity/Proof/FtsGuessWork.lean) bounds secret tests by the retained signing and verification work on every supported translated execution. An external hash request adds at most one test, and signing disclosures add none. [FtsGuessBudget.lean](SphincsSecurity/Proof/FtsGuessBudget.lean) identifies the completed verdict and boundary record with the existing reference experiment and transfers the original hash-query bound to the continuing interpreter. For every supported auxiliary seed and completed run, its retained work $C$ and test counter $T$ satisfy $1212415+C\le q$ and $T\le C$; the constant is the original key-generation debit. This holds for the fixed-secret and lazy original-reference laws. Applying the same budget to forced-test laws will require proving that their supported histories remain supported by these laws. The next steps are the witness-to-guess implications and the two remaining probability bounds.
