# Current 127-bit proof status

The public theorem remains 126 bits. The original-game 127-bit bound is established for every $q\ge3\cdot2^{114}$; the smaller positive-budget interval remains open. This is the status entry point; the other notes supply mathematical derivations rather than a chronological progress log.

Put $N=2^{128}$, $x=q/N$, $\delta=11/65536$ and $\epsilon(q)=q/2^{169}+2^{-700}$. The [closing contract](minimal-closing-contract.md) targets $2x-x^2+\delta x+\epsilon(q)$ for $3\cdot2^{114}\le q<2^{127}$ and $(15/8+\delta)x+\epsilon(q)$ below that split. Both suffice for the 127-bit slope. Budgets at least $2^{127}$ use probability at most one.

## Remaining work

1. Reserve encoding, other structural and native message charges alongside the OTS charges in the common original budget. Prove completed-witness estimates including preparation in either chronological order, preserving the additional monitoring observations they need. The counter transport through the conditional samples and the aggregate ideal OTS-query bound are proved.
2. Establish fresh-message and signing kernels in each forced-FTS law. Use the record-and-length projection, attach that law's own proposal words, and bound one true guess with a near certificate plus two distinct guesses.
3. Combine the small-range bound with `security127_of_large_budget`. Audit the resulting public theorem without additional cryptographic premises.

The [small-range gate audit](small-range-gate-audit.md) specifies why secret-dependent canonical high halves cannot enter the OTS history, and why the forced-FTS comparison needs no joint posterior for rejected proposal values. The full-cache exceptional flag is also excluded from that projected stopping rule: its probability is paid on the original law using the spent-call invariant.

## Shortcuts that do not close the current estimates

Two tempting numerical shortcuts do not remove the small-budget obligation with the current estimates. First, crediting the established honest-work debits is insufficient. At $q=2^{112}$, $x=2^{-16}$ and $\delta=11/2^{16}$, the unshifted excess $\delta x-x^2$ is $10/2^{32}$ before exceptions. Even replacing $x$ in the primitive term by $(q-K)/2^{128}$ with $K=1212415+2^{24}\cdot28504$ leaves excess greater than $9/2^{32}$. This calculation grants the minimum debit for every allowed signing request; it is a generous arithmetic check, not a valid uniform debit for executions making fewer requests. Reclaiming key generation alone also fails this check. A sharper bound tied to the actual adaptive allocation would need a new argument.

Second, the existing terminal-price moments cannot justify replacing a correlated weighted price by its mean. For an abstract nonnegative price $P$ equal to $2$ with probability $10^{-4}$ and zero otherwise, $\mathbb E P\le1/5$ and $\operatorname{Var}(P)\le13/25000$. Taking a bounded charge $M=q\mathbf1_{P=2}$ nevertheless gives $\mathbb E[MP]=2\mathbb E[M]$. This is a counterexample to an inference from the moment bounds alone, not an attack on the scheme or a claim about its exact price distribution. The terminal-price excess term accounts for precisely this possible dependence; removing it requires more than an average-price estimate.

Sharpening the exact terminal-price tail alone cannot eliminate the small-budget argument either. In the existing uniform proposal word, put $m=2^{26}$, $L=25313293$, and $P=2^{-48}\sum_i R_i^{14}$, where $R_i$ is the number of occurrences of index $i$. Let $A_i$ mean $R_i=12$. The binomial and multinomial formulas, followed by Bernoulli's inequality and the first two inclusion-exclusion terms, give

\[
\Pr\Bigl[\bigcup_i A_i\Bigr]\ge m\frac{(L-11)^{12}}{12!m^{12}}\left(1-\frac{L-12}{m}\right)-\binom m2\frac{L^{24}}{(12!)^2m^{24}}>2^{-21}.
\]

On this event $P\ge12^{14}/2^{48}>4$, so $\mathbb E[(P-2)_+]>2^{-20}$. The two strict numerical inequalities were checked with exact rational arithmetic; this is a paper calculation, not a new Lean theorem. Even allowing a baseline payment of $2$ instead of $1$ therefore leaves a positive excess in this terminal-price estimate. At $q=2^{100}$, its coefficient exceeds $x=2^{-28}$, so a bound of the form $2x-x^2+\eta x$ with this excess coefficient $\eta$ does not close. This concerns the analytical proposal envelope, not an attack or a lower bound on forgery probability. A sharper joint allocation argument could still improve the result; computing higher moments of this same envelope alone cannot remove the separate small-budget obligation.

## Budget transfer using a prefix cap

The cap stops a prefix program before its $(q+1)$st query to the selected prefix. `QueryCap.run_queryBound` proves the syntactic bound required by `realRun_empty_cost_lower`. `QueryCap.run_eq_counted` identifies its distribution exactly: complete counted runs that exceed the budget map to the ordinary cap result, retaining all probability mass. `seedGame_counted_le` bounds the selected-prefix count by the original hash-cost trace, including verifier work. Consequently `realRun_cap_erased` recovers the uncapped real output distribution after removing the cap's remaining-budget field. `realRun_cap_recover_count` also recovers the joint distribution of the original result and its prefix-query count by subtracting the remaining budget from the initial budget.

For $q<N$, the capped likelihood lower bound has positive coefficient $1-q/N$. Its pointwise form puts the entire ideal support inside real support, including the cap result and observed prefix table. `idealRun_cap_valid` therefore excludes cap termination and bounds every completed ideal result's original cost by $q$. `prefixCappedSeedGame_contract` instantiates this argument for the concrete source using only the original hash bound, the budget range and membership of the fixed samples in their source supports.

This establishes the bounded conditional interface without an operational cap inside omitted private signing work. `referenceRecordedGame_allocation_le` and `referenceRecordedGame_expected_allocation_le` supply one original-game budget for the sum over all OTS chains. `game_counted_source` identifies each fixed-oracle interpreter count with its charge in the common program. `prefixCountedObservedGame_original` completes the counter transport through conditional seed sampling. `prefixIdealCostGame_shared_budget` then gives $(1-q/N)\sum_c\mathbb E Q_c^{\rm ideal}\le q$ by averaging the capped likelihood comparison and applying the common original budget. Allocation of the other query classes and the completed-witness probability estimates remain to be connected. The original scheme and budget definition remain unchanged.
