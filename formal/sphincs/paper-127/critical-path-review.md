# Current 127-bit proof status

The public theorem remains 126 bits. The original-game 127-bit bound is established for every $q\ge3\cdot2^{114}$; the smaller positive-budget interval remains open. This is the status entry point; the other notes supply mathematical derivations rather than a chronological progress log.

Put $N=2^{128}$, $x=q/N$, $\delta=11/65536$ and $\epsilon(q)=q/2^{169}+2^{-700}$. The [closing contract](minimal-closing-contract.md) targets $2x-x^2+\delta x+\epsilon(q)$ for $3\cdot2^{114}\le q<2^{127}$ and $(15/8+\delta)x+\epsilon(q)$ below that split. Both suffice for the 127-bit slope. Budgets at least $2^{127}$ use probability at most one.

## First contact with prepared suffixes

[PartialChainSuffix](../SphincsSecurity/Proof/PartialChainSuffix.lean) bounds the endpoint density $W(T,Y)$ by one plus the number of nonempty, fully queried suffix paths ending at $Y$. [PartialChainCompletion](../SphincsSecurity/Proof/PartialChainCompletion.lean) counts every completed suffix by its starting queried row. Thus completed suffixes plus pending queried rows equal the number of distinct queried rows.

Let $P(T)$ be the pending count. Before any contact with $Y$, recording one consistent row gives $W(T',Y)+P(T')\le P(T)+2$. This pays for a final query that completes several previously prepared paths. [PartialChainLastRow](../SphincsSecurity/Proof/PartialChainLastRow.lean) proves that the pending count after a last-level query is independent of its answer, and that the density is a martingale under a lazy row observation.

[PartialChainContactPotential](../SphincsSecurity/Proof/PartialChainContactPotential.lean) uses potential $P(T)/N$ before contact and $W(T,Y)$ afterward. Its expected increase is at most $2/N$ per prefix query. [AdaptiveChainPotential](../SphincsSecurity/Proof/AdaptiveChainPotential.lean) sums such drift against the actual expected query counter, allowing adaptive queries and auxiliary randomness. The exact likelihood identity then gives `realRun_contact_le`: $\Pr_R[\mathrm{contact}]\le2\mathbb E_I Q/N$.

[AdaptiveChainCapObservation](../SphincsSecurity/Proof/AdaptiveChainCapObservation.lean) now identifies the uncapped and capped real contact events while preserving their observed prefix rows. [QueryCapBalance](../SphincsSecurity/Proof/QueryCapBalance.lean) accounts for every query through the cap, and [AdaptiveChainCapContact](../SphincsSecurity/Proof/AdaptiveChainCapContact.lean) identifies the completed ideal run's explicit counter with its spent-budget field. [OtsPrefixContactProbability](../SphincsSecurity/Proof/OtsPrefixContactProbability.lean) averages this bound over the actual conditional source samples and retains the encoding, other-hash and native message reservation when summing over chains.

[AdaptiveChainRestart](../SphincsSecurity/Proof/AdaptiveChainRestart.lean) also allows a nonempty initial partial table with no contact with the fixed endpoint. If $a$ is its distinct queried-row count and $Q^+$ counts future queries, the lemma proves $\mathbb E_I[W_{\rm final}\mathbf1_{\rm contact}]\le\mathbb E_I[a+2Q^+]/N$. Its companion lemma transfers this charge using the likelihood lower bound on the final observed table. These are fixed-endpoint weighted kernels; applying them at original transcript stops still requires the corresponding conditional observation law. The two-edge, distinct-chain contact and marker estimates, their original-game witness classification, and the forced-FTS estimates remain open.

## Two-edge transition bounds and their accumulation

The one-chain counter $K$ in [PartialChainContactCount](../SphincsSecurity/Proof/PartialChainContactCount.lean) counts distinct queried last-level rows returning the endpoint. It does not count contacted chains. Fresh last rows increment it with probability $1/N$, and repeated rows leave it unchanged. [AdaptiveChainBudgetPotential](../SphincsSecurity/Proof/AdaptiveChainBudgetPotential.lean) supports a potential indexed by remaining query budget. Applying it to $K(K-1)+2bK/N$ proves the factorial moment under adaptive allocation and stopping. [AdaptiveChainContactMoments](../SphincsSecurity/Proof/AdaptiveChainContactMoments.lean) combines this with the first moment to obtain the exact correction required by the paper calculation.

[PartialChainTwoEdge](../SphincsSecurity/Proof/PartialChainTwoEdge.lean) classifies the first completion and proves that, before it, every nonempty fully queried suffix to the endpoint is a last-edge hit. Thus $W\le1+K$. [PartialChainRowCharge](../SphincsSecurity/Proof/PartialChainRowCharge.lean) proves that a row update adds at most the incoming prefix density, then bounds that density by one plus its fully queried suffix paths. This gives the weighted first-completion bounds in both chronological orders.

The following accumulation route is a proof plan, not an established Lean theorem. Let $D$ count completed suffix paths of length at least two to any endpoint, let $P$ count queried last-row inputs reached by queried penultimate rows, and let $R$ count completed suffixes of length at least two in the prefix obtained by removing the last level. A fresh last query adds its earlier-path count to $D$, and adds one to $P$ when productive. A fresh penultimate query adds its earlier-path count to $R$. These counts are monotone under consistent observations. With $s$ selected queries spent, use the accumulated charge $A=D+2P+sK(K+2)+KR$. Its terminal baseline obeys $D+2P\le3Q/2$, and $s,R\le q$ would give $A\le3Q/2+q[K(K-1)+4K]$.

The needed local compensation inequality compares the increase of $A/N$ with the expected weighted first-completion mass. After completion, the density martingale supplies the corresponding equality for the bad-event weight. Telescoping this comparison would avoid introducing a separate stopping program for the one-chain estimate. The monotone path-count increments, the $3/2$ terminal bound and this compensation argument still need Lean proofs before the two-edge probability estimate can be used.

## Shortcuts that do not close the current estimates

Two tempting numerical shortcuts do not remove the small-budget obligation with the current estimates. First, crediting the established honest-work debits is insufficient. At $q=2^{112}$, $x=2^{-16}$ and $\delta=11/2^{16}$, the unshifted excess $\delta x-x^2$ is $10/2^{32}$ before exceptions. Even replacing $x$ in the primitive term by $(q-K)/2^{128}$ with $K=1212415+2^{24}\cdot28504$ leaves excess greater than $9/2^{32}$. This calculation grants the minimum debit for every allowed signing request; it is a generous arithmetic check, not a valid uniform debit for executions making fewer requests. Reclaiming key generation alone also fails this check. A sharper bound tied to the actual adaptive allocation would need a new argument.

Second, the existing terminal-price moments cannot justify replacing a correlated weighted price by its mean. For an abstract nonnegative price $P$ equal to $2$ with probability $10^{-4}$ and zero otherwise, $\mathbb E P\le1/5$ and $\operatorname{Var}(P)\le13/25000$. Taking a bounded charge $M=q\mathbf1_{P=2}$ nevertheless gives $\mathbb E[MP]=2\mathbb E[M]$. This is a counterexample to an inference from the moment bounds alone, not an attack on the scheme or a claim about its exact price distribution. The terminal-price excess term accounts for precisely this possible dependence; removing it requires more than an average-price estimate.

Sharpening the exact terminal-price tail alone cannot eliminate the small-budget argument either. In the existing uniform proposal word, put $m=2^{26}$, $L=25313293$, and $P=2^{-48}\sum_i R_i^{14}$, where $R_i$ is the number of occurrences of index $i$. Let $A_i$ mean $R_i=12$. The binomial and multinomial formulas, followed by Bernoulli's inequality and the first two inclusion-exclusion terms, give

\[
\Pr\Bigl[\bigcup_i A_i\Bigr]\ge m\frac{(L-11)^{12}}{12!m^{12}}\left(1-\frac{L-12}{m}\right)-\binom m2\frac{L^{24}}{(12!)^2m^{24}}>2^{-21}.
\]

On this event $P\ge12^{14}/2^{48}>4$, so $\mathbb E[(P-2)_+]>2^{-20}$. The two strict numerical inequalities were checked with exact rational arithmetic; this is a paper calculation, not a new Lean theorem. Even allowing a baseline payment of $2$ instead of $1$ therefore leaves a positive excess in this terminal-price estimate. At $q=2^{100}$, its coefficient exceeds $x=2^{-28}$, so a bound of the form $2x-x^2+\eta x$ with this excess coefficient $\eta$ does not close. This concerns the analytical proposal envelope, not an attack or a lower bound on forgery probability. A sharper joint allocation argument could still improve the result; computing higher moments of this same envelope alone cannot remove the separate small-budget obligation.

## Budget transfer using a prefix cap

The cap stops a prefix program before its $(q+1)$st query to the selected prefix. `QueryCap.run_queryBound` proves the syntactic bound required by `realRun_empty_cost_lower`. `QueryCap.run_eq_counted` identifies its distribution exactly: complete counted runs that exceed the budget map to the ordinary cap result, retaining all probability mass. `seedGame_counted_le` bounds the selected-prefix count by the original hash-cost trace, including verifier work. Consequently `realRun_cap_erased` recovers the uncapped real output distribution after removing the cap's remaining-budget field. `realRun_cap_recover_count` also recovers the joint distribution of the original result and its prefix-query count by subtracting the remaining budget from the initial budget.

For $q<N$, the capped likelihood lower bound has positive coefficient $1-q/N$. Its pointwise form puts the entire ideal support inside real support, including the cap result and observed prefix table. `idealRun_cap_valid` therefore excludes cap termination and bounds every completed ideal result's original cost by $q$. `prefixCappedSeedGame_contract` instantiates this argument for the concrete source using only the original hash bound, the budget range and membership of the fixed samples in their source supports.

This establishes the bounded conditional interface without an operational cap inside omitted private signing work. `referenceRecordedGame_allocation_le` and `referenceRecordedGame_expected_allocation_le` supply one original-game budget for the sum over all OTS chains. `game_counted_source` identifies each fixed-oracle interpreter count with its charge in the common program. `prefixCountedObservedGame_original` completes the counter transport through conditional seed sampling. `prefixIdealCostGame_shared_budget` then gives $(1-q/N)\sum_c\mathbb E Q_c^{\rm ideal}\le q$ by averaging the capped likelihood comparison and applying the common original budget. `prefixIdealCostGame_joint_budget` retains the original expected encoding, other non-message and native message charges beside the scaled ideal OTS cost. Its external charges count all calls, providing an upper bound for the later fresh-query subsets. The concrete first-contact probability bound now uses this allocation with observed-row cap erasure. The fixed-endpoint restart charge includes earlier preparation. Its application at original transcript stops, the other completed-witness estimates and their monitoring observations remain to be connected. The original scheme and budget definition remain unchanged.
